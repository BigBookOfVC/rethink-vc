ABOUT THE EPISODE

Until recently, conversations about venture capital in India often centered on consumer apps, affordable data, and billion-user growth stories: payments, e-commerce, food delivery. But another narrative has been taking shape.

In just a few years, Leo has expanded across Asia, the US, and Europe, developing a perspective on how Indian talent is contributing globally. They call it Global Indian Alpha. At first glance, it may sound like branding. In practice, it reflects how founders of Indian origin are increasingly building across borders.

  • LambdaTest. A company that adapted quickly with new AI-led tools.
  • Atoa Payments. Applying lessons from India's UPI system to the UK's open banking space.
  • A portfolio now spanning more than 70 companies across India, the US, and Europe.

This isn't about hype cycles. It's about creating a platform that can meet a founder in San Francisco on Monday and their co-founder in Bangalore on Tuesday, in person, not just on Zoom. Few funds are structured for that kind of cross-border reach.

The Indian startup story many know is still largely domestic: consumer adoption, supportive policy, IPO activity. What Leo highlights is a next chapter: India not just as a market, but as a global source of talent and ideas. With a diaspora of 40M people, second only to the US in unicorn founder count, more companies are being built with cross-border teams from day one.

This isn't the cliché of "India is the next China". It's a quieter, more durable reality: operational efficiency honed in Bangalore, scaled in Silicon Valley, and applied in London. Venture often speaks in abstractions, moats, founder-first, operating edge. What's happening here is more grounded: turning dispersed talent into global outcomes.
